    GNC Team Learning Paths

    Why This Matters

    Controls is a difficult subject that requires a lot of prerequisite knowledge to meaningfully contribute to a project such as the jet vanes rocket we're building. The GNC (Guidance, Navigation, and Control) team works on complex rocket control systems that demand interdisciplinary knowledge spanning mathematics, engineering, and computer science.

    Taking the time to learn these fundamentals will benefit you not only as a member of GNC, but also as a student and for your resume. At Georgia Tech, the traditional path to controls knowledge requires taking PHYS 2211, COE 2001, AE 2220/MATH 2552, AE 3530, and finally AE 3531 - meaning it takes at least two years from physics to reach control theory. This bootcamp condenses that knowledge into focused, practical learning paths.

    Team Structure & Collaboration

    We work as a specialized team where different members can focus on different areas:

    • Controls people assume they are working with an accurate state estimate
    • State estimation people don't worry about how to orient the rocket, merely determining the rocket's orientation at a given time
    • Software people bridge hardware and controls, making algorithms actually run
    • Avionics people design and build the electronic systems that power everything

    This division allows for specialization while maintaining system-level thinking.

    🎯 Learning Strategy Recommendations

    Universal Principles

    • Take detailed notes - Keep a technical notebook or digital documentation
    • Implement in code - Don't just watch videos, write and test examples
    • Ask for guidance - Engage with team leads, but work to figure out answers yourself
    • Start small projects - Apply knowledge immediately to reinforce learning

    Time Commitment Options

    • Intensive: 10-15 hours/week → Complete primary path in one semester
    • Balanced: 5-8 hours/week → Complete over two semesters
    • Casual: 2-4 hours/week → Complete over academic year

    Success Factors

    Your learning is a function of how much effort you put into it. Team members who actively engage with leads, take good notes, and implement concepts in code learn exponentially faster than those who try to go it alone.

    Remember: These paths are designed to stand alone, but understanding the basics of multiple areas makes you a more effective team member and better prepared for industry roles.
